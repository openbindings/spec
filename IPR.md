# Intellectual-property status

This page describes the current, limited intellectual-property posture of the
OpenBindings working draft. It is not legal advice and does not imply rights
that have not actually been granted.

## In force today

The specification repository is licensed under
[Apache License 2.0](LICENSE). Unless a contribution explicitly states
otherwise, Apache 2.0 §5 treats an intentionally submitted contribution as
being under that license. Apache 2.0 §3 defines the patent license a
contributor grants for patent claims necessarily infringed by their
contribution alone or by its combination with the work.

That is the complete project-specific patent commitment currently in force.
OpenBindings does **not** currently have a separately executed
standards-essential-claims policy, contributor patent agreement, or
implementer-wide royalty-free patent promise. Implementers must not infer one
from the words “open standard,” from participation in a discussion, or from
the absence of a disclosure.

## Disclosures

Anyone with actual knowledge of a patent or published patent application they
believe may contain claims essential to implementing a normative OpenBindings
requirement is asked to disclose:

- the patent or application number;
- the affected specification, revision, and section; and
- whether the holder has stated licensing terms.

Send a disclosure to `hello@openbindings.com`. Non-confidential disclosures
will be recorded publicly in this file or a linked ledger. This request does
not require a patent search and creates no representation that the project has
performed one.

As of 2026-07-24, the editor has received no such disclosure. This dated
statement is a report about received disclosures, not a non-infringement
assurance.

## Decision required before the 0.2 release

Before publishing final 0.2 releases, the editors must make and record an
informed choice about contributor-time and final-specification patent
commitments. The leading candidate is the Open Web Foundation model:

- OWF CLA 1.0 (Copyright and Patent) during specification development; and
- OWFa 1.0, or its patent-only form when copyright remains under Apache 2.0,
  for each identified final specification.

Adoption requires review appropriate to a legal commitment, an explicit
acceptance mechanism, and recorded assent from every person or entity whose
commitment is claimed. Merely linking those forms or merging this page does
not execute them, and the project will not apply a new commitment
retroactively by implication.

Until that decision is completed, release materials must describe the precise
Apache 2.0 posture above rather than claiming comprehensive
standards-essential patent assurance.
